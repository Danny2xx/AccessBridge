## Register

brand

The story, evidence and ask sections are brand surfaces. The Explore tool
inside them follows product conventions: familiar controls, fast feedback, no
decorative motion.

## Users

- **Competition judges and the UKREiiF final panel.** Built-environment and
  real-estate professionals, plus B-KQ partners such as Aston University,
  Birmingham City Council, Birmingham City University, Bruntwood SciTech and
  Woodbourne Group. They watch on a laptop or a projector, often straight
  after the Phase 1 SpineLens site, and have minutes, not hours. The brief
  asks for "the art of the possible" rather than technical detail.
- **Council and transport officers** who want to check the numbers and try
  their own budgets after the session.
- **Local community groups** in Nechells, Aston, Saltley and nearby areas who
  want to know what it means for them.

## Product Purpose

AccessBridge AI is Phase 2 of the Innovation Spine, the £10m "Make It Usable"
stage. It asks where new and redesigned stops would do the most for the people
who need them most. It combines real deprivation, population and stop data with
an optimiser, then shows the result, the trade-offs and the evidence status of
every figure. Success means a non-technical viewer can say, in one sentence,
who gains, by how much, for what cost, and how sure we are.

## Brand Personality

Evidence-led, candid, civic.

Plain English first, number second. It names real places, not neighbourhood
codes. It is confident about what the data shows and open about what it does
not. It is a sibling of SpineLens Phase 1, not a copy: the same story, the
next chapter.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS analytics dashboards: hero KPI tiles, gradient accents, numbers
  with no explanation.
- Academic GIS output: unexplained jargon such as LSOA, D1 or decile, legends
  that assume training.
- Glossy property marketing that hides caveats or presents placeholder costs
  as quotes.

## Design Principles

1. **Say what it means, then show the number.** Every figure comes with a
   sentence a resident would understand.
2. **Label the evidence.** Every figure says whether it is measured data, our
   modelling or a placeholder estimate.
3. **Name real places and real people.** Use place names and resident counts,
   never area codes on their own.
4. **Show the trade-offs, not only the wins.** Who is not helped, and why, is
   part of the story.
5. **One story across phases.** The site reads as the next chapter of the
   Innovation Spine after Phase 1.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA as the floor. This is an equity project, so it practises what it
preaches. The deprivation scale is ordered by lightness so it survives colour
blindness, and colour is never the only cue. Every map view has a written
description next to it. Story steps and the Explore controls work from the
keyboard. Camera fly-throughs and reveals respect reduced-motion settings.
